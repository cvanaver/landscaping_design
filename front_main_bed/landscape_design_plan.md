# Front Main Bed — Landscape Design Plan

A four-row, part-shade redesign of the front main bed at **1013 Colfax St, Evanston IL** (USDA Zone 5b–6a). Keeps the existing healthy structure (yews, daffodils, geranium, alliums), adds a clean evergreen midline (boxwood), introduces a seasonal flower band (TBD pending pH), and finishes with a foliage ribbon (heuchera) along the front. Replaces the failed 2020 Chalet planting palette.

---

## 1. Site Summary

**Location:** 1013 Colfax St, Evanston, IL — USDA Zone 5b–6a
**Aspect:** North-facing. Bed is shaded by the house through most of the day; west end is shadier still (tree canopy), east end is brighter (open to south near the stone retaining wall).
**Light read:** **Part shade overall.** West end ~2–4 hrs direct/dappled; east end ~4–6 hrs.
**Soil:** Workable clay, no rock layer noted at this site. **pH suspected alkaline**; test pending (Luster Leaf Rapitest 1601 ordered).
**Bed envelope (working rectangle):** **13 ft × 13 ft** (156" × 156")

> 💡 The real lawn-facing front edge is curved, not straight. The 13'×13' rectangle is the *planning envelope* used for spacing math. The curved front edge is a finish detail — it doesn't change plant positions, only the edge of the mulch.

**Boundaries**

| Side | Boundary |
|---|---|
| Back (north) | House wall |
| East | Stone retaining wall (steps into front entry pavers, terminates at the lawn-facing face) |
| West | Design boundary: porch + mature tree + surviving hydrangea (leave-alone zone) |
| Front (south) | Lawn |

**Existing kept conditions**

- **Yews along the back/house wall.** Healthy, currently extending ~5–5.5 ft into bed depth. Plan calls for a **light shaping prune in March** to define a cleaner ~5 ft front face. Keep all of them; do not remove.
- **Max Frei geranium** (purple-pink low spreader): 2–3 surviving clusters at the **east end** near the stone wall. Keep, may divide in year 2.
- **Millennium alliums** (purple drumstick, July–August bloom): some of original 4 survived, exact count TBD. Keep what's there.
- **Existing daffodils:** substantial mass at east end (in dieback phase mid-spring). Keep, do not relocate.
- The middle and parts of the east bed: degraded since 2020 install (see § 9, Gotchas).

## 2. Design Concept

**Four-row layered planting.** Reading from the house wall out to the lawn:

```
   HOUSE WALL  (back / north)
   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  STONE
   |                                                       |  WALL
   |   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  |  (east)
   |   ░░░░░░░  YEWS (kept, prune to clean 5' front)  ░░  |
   |   ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  |
   |                                                       |
   |   ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ●  ← Row 2: 10 boxwood   |
   |                                   ('Green Velvet')    |
   |                                                       |
   |     *  *  *  *  *  *  *  ← Row 3: 7 TBD tall flowers  |
   |     • • •   • •  • • •   (drifted bulbs threaded      |
   |          • • •   • • •    through this band — 40)     |
   |                                                       |
   |   ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲ ▲      G G G   ← Row 4a (W 10'):    |
   |   Heuchera 'Plum Royale'  Allium    9 heuchera        |
   |   (9 @ 14" o.c.)          + Max     Row 4b (E ~3'):   |
   |                           Frei      existing mass     |
   |________________________________________________________|
        LAWN  (front / south)
```

**Why this design works**

- **Structure first, color second.** Yews (back) + boxwood (mid) = an evergreen frame that reads well 12 months a year. The flowering layer sits *inside* a frame that already looks intentional in winter.
- **Persistent foliage as the visual baseline.** Heuchera 'Plum Royale' holds burgundy color from April through hard freeze. It screens the seasonal mess of Row 3 (bulb dieback, post-bloom flower stalks).
- **Part-shade plant palette.** Every species was chosen for the actual light conditions (north-facing, 2–6 hrs). The 2020 Chalet install failed largely because it used sun-bed plants (coneflower, calamintha) here.
- **Bulb succession through Row 3.** Four bulb varieties bloom April → early May, before Row 3 perennials hit stride. The flowering perennials emerge and hide the dying bulb foliage.
- **East end is left alone.** Daffodils + alliums + Max Frei are already a self-layered mass. Cramming heuchera in there would fight the existing planting for no gain.

## 5. Soil Strategy

The west fence bed had a buried-rock problem; **this bed does not**. Soil here is workable clay. The risk profile is different:

1. **Compacted clay → poor drainage** is the likely failure mode here, not rocks.
2. **Suspected alkaline pH** — confirmed once kit arrives. Drives the Row 3 species choice.
3. **Possible foundation runoff** along the back (house wall) — soil near the wall may be more alkaline than mid-bed.

### Per-hole protocol

1. Dig hole **2× the root ball width**, same depth as root ball.
2. Loosen the sides of the hole (don't leave glazed clay walls — roots can't penetrate).
3. Mix excavated soil **50/50 with high-quality compost** (Leaf-Gro, Mushroom Compost, or Chalet's compost blend).
4. For boxwood and heuchera: optionally add a small handful of slow-release fertilizer (Plant-tone or similar) to the backfill mix.
5. Set plant with **crown at or slightly above grade — never buried**. This is the #1 mistake from the 2020 install.
6. Backfill, firm gently with hands (don't stomp), build a small water-ring berm around the plant.
7. Water in immediately and deeply.

### Compost & mulch quantities (whole bed)

| Material | Estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Compost (for per-hole mix) | ~4 cu ft | One 2-cu-ft bag per ~12 holes |
| Shredded hardwood mulch | ~1.5 cu yd | Or 9–10 bags of 2 cu ft. 2–3" deep across bed |
| Bulb fertilizer (bone meal or Bulb-tone) | 1 small bag | Sprinkle in bulb planting holes in October |

## 6. Install Sequence

Phased so that the irreversible big moves happen before fussier work, and so that pH-gated decisions can wait without holding up the whole install.

### Day 0 — Prep (March, before new growth)

1. **Light shaping prune on yews.** Hand pruners + hedge shears. Target a clean ~5 ft front face. Stop if you're cutting into bare wood — yews are slow to regenerate from old wood.
2. **Mark the working rectangle** (13'×13') with mason's line and stakes. Confirm corners against the house wall and the stone wall.
3. **Pull dead 2020-planting debris** (old hydrangea stumps, dead coneflower crowns). Don't disturb the surviving daffodil bulbs at the east end.
4. **Take pH samples** as soon as the test kit arrives. 3–4 spots (west, middle, east, near foundation), let dry, run the test.

### Day 1 — Install structure (boxwood + heuchera)

5. Lay out boxwood centers along Row 2 with flags. Confirm 15" spacing and 18" offset from yew face. Step back and sight down the line.
6. Plant the 10 boxwood per the per-hole protocol in § 5.
7. Lay out heuchera centers along the western 10 ft of Row 4a. Confirm 14" spacing.
8. Plant the 9 heuchera per per-hole protocol.
9. Water everything in deeply.

### Day 2 (or later, once pH result is in) — Row 3 flowers

10. Lay out the 7 Row 3 centers with flags (22" o.c., 36" off yew face). The flag positions are independent of species — they don't change based on the pH result.
11. Buy and plant the chosen Row 3 species (astilbe / anemone / drifted split — see [open_decisions.md](open_decisions.md) #1).
12. Water in.

### Day 3 — Mulch

13. Spread **2–3" of shredded hardwood mulch** across the whole bed.
14. **Pull mulch back ~2" from the base of every plant** (no volcano mulching).
15. Skim mulch lightly around the existing daffodil mass — don't bury foliage that's still dying back.

### Late October / early November — Bulbs

16. Plant the 40 bulbs in irregular drifted clusters of 3–7 through the Row 3 band. Use the Ruby script's bulb layout as a guide.
17. Mix a pinch of Bulb-tone in each cluster.
18. Top off mulch over the bulb-planting disturbance.

### Year 1 watering (April – September)

- First 4 weeks: deep water 2–3× per week.
- Weeks 5–12: 1× per week, deep.
- After mid-July: only if no rain for 7+ days. Tapering teaches roots to seek depth.

## 8. Year 2–3 Outlook

By year 2:

- Boxwood reads as a continuous **wavy mid-line** (not yet a tight hedge — that takes 4–5 years from #2 stock).
- Heuchera fills its 10 ft ribbon completely; foliage mounds touch.
- Row 3 perennials are at mature size; bloom is reliable.
- Bulb succession (April → early May) is well-established.

By year 3:

- Boxwood begins to look like a proper low hedge. Light shearing in March to maintain the line.
- Heuchera may need division (every 3–4 years). Lift in early spring, split, replant or share.
- Re-evaluate Row 3: if any plants are sulking, swap individually rather than overhauling.

## 9. Gotchas (lessons from the 2020 install)

The 2020 Chalet install lost most of its showy perennials within 4 years (8 Bobo Hydrangeas → 6 dead, 6 coneflowers → 0, 5 calamintha → 0, 3 anemone → 0, 1 chokeberry → 0). Three likely root causes, each addressed in this redesign:

1. **Sun-bed plants in a part-shade bed.**
   Coneflower and calamintha want 6+ hrs sun. This bed gets 2–6. They were doomed.
   → *This redesign:* every species (boxwood, heuchera, astilbe/anemone) is rated for part shade.

2. **Possible alkaline pH intolerance.**
   Hydrangea chlorosis (yellowing leaves with green veins) is a classic alkaline-soil symptom. The mass loss of hydrangeas points at pH.
   → *This redesign:* Row 3 species is gated on a pH test before buying anything. See [open_decisions.md](open_decisions.md) #1.

3. **Install-quality issues — planting too deep or J-rooted nursery stock.**
   When plants die uniformly within 1–2 years, the cause is usually install protocol or rootbound nursery stock, not random plant fade.
   → *This redesign:* explicit per-hole protocol (§ 5) and inspection criteria at the nursery ([buy_list_and_sourcing.md](buy_list_and_sourcing.md)).

### Other gotchas

- **Boxwood blight.** Real disease, but 'Green Velvet' has decent resistance. Don't water overhead; water at the base. Don't plant in already-blighted soil.
- **Volcano mulching boxwood.** Boxwood roots are shallow; piling mulch against the trunk smothers them. 2" away.
- **Cutting heuchera back in fall.** Don't. Leave the foliage on through winter — it's evergreen-ish and protects the crown. Trim damaged leaves in March only.
- **Tulip perennialization.** Even Darwin Hybrids fade after 3–4 years. Plan to top up tulip count every 3rd fall.
- **Don't plant on top of the existing daffodil mass at the east end.** It's invisible mid-summer; the bulbs are there. Stay west of x=120 for new heuchera.
