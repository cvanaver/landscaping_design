# Buy List — West Fence Landscape Install

One-page consolidated shopping list. Take this to the nursery.

### What to look for at the nursery

**Karl Foerster:**
- ✅ Multiple stiff green blades emerging from crown
- ✅ Roots fill the pot but aren't a tight white spiral
- ✅ No yellowing or brown bases
- ❌ Avoid pots where soil pulls away from the edge — drought-stressed

**Echinacea (coneflower):**
- ✅ Multiple stems from base (not a single stem — that's a young plug)
- ✅ Dark green foliage, no leaf spot or yellowing
- ✅ A few buds OK; full bloom not necessary (plants in heavy bloom transplant worse)
- ❌ Avoid plants flopping or with mushy crowns

**Geranium macrorrhizum:**
- ✅ Compact mound of scalloped leaves
- ✅ Fragrant when leaves are lightly bruised (the "Bigroot" smell)
- ✅ Visible rhizome at soil line (the thick "bigroot" — that's the spread vehicle)
- ❌ Avoid plants with yellowing lower leaves or sparse foliage

## Soil amendments

| Item | Qty | Where | Approx cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mushroom compost** (or aged manure compost) | ~30 cu ft (15 bags of 2 cu ft, or ¾–1 yd bulk) | Nursery or landscape supply | $60–90 bagged / $30–40 bulk |
| **Leaf mold or peat moss** (10% of backfill mix) | 4 cu ft (2 bags) | Garden center | $20 |
| **Slow-release organic fertilizer** (Plant-tone or Espoma 4-3-3) | 1× 8 lb bag | Garden center | $25 |

⚠️ **Do NOT buy:** cheap bagged "compost" from big-box stores (mostly bark fines, low CEC). Synthetic 10-10-10. Dyed-red cypress mulch. Cocoa hull mulch (dog-toxic).

## Mulch

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shredded hardwood mulch** (oak / maple bark, natural color) | ~3 cu yards bulk, or ~18 bags of 2 cu ft | 3" depth across entire bed footprint |

Bulk delivery is usually cheaper if you can take 2+ yards. ~$30–40/yd delivered locally vs. ~$5/bag.

## Hardware & tools

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| Marking flags or marking spray paint | 1 pack of 50 flags | Plant centers + edge line |
| Flat spade | 1 | Edge cut |
| Round-point shovel | 1 | Hole digging |
| **Broadfork** | 1 (optional, recommended) | For aerating the pear zone bed-wide. Borrow if you can — $150 to buy. |
| Wheelbarrow | 1 | Backfill mixing |
| 5-gal bucket(s) | 2 | Pre-filled with water near bed for hand-watering |
| Hose with shower wand or watering can | 1 | Initial watering |
| **Soaker hose**, 50 ft | 1 | Year-1 irrigation. Bury under mulch. |
| Hose Y-splitter | 1 | So soaker doesn't tie up the spigot |
| Rain gauge | 1 | Sanity-check actual rainfall vs. weather apps |
| Long screwdriver | 1 (probably already own) | The "screwdriver test" for moisture |

⚠️ **Do NOT buy:** rolled plastic landscape edging. Heaves out of sandy soil in 1–2 winters. If you want edging, use steel or composite. No edging is also fine — the spade-cut edge holds for a year.

⚠️ **Do NOT buy:** a rock bar or digging bar. The original plan called for one. The soil is sand below 10" — you don't need it.

## Where to buy (Chicago North Shore)

In order of recommendation:

1. **Chalet Nursery (Wilmette)** — closest, best plant quality, premium pricing. All three plants should be in stock during planting season.
2. **Pesche's Flowers / Garden Center (Des Plaines)** — strong perennial selection, often has named geranium cultivars Chalet doesn't.
3. **Gethsemane Garden Center (Andersonville)** — strong perennial selection, worth the drive for cultivar variety.
4. **Possibility Place Nursery (Monee)** — natives specialist, best for *Echinacea purpurea* straight species in bulk if buying a lot. Worth the drive if you're getting all 27 here.
5. **Big-box (Home Depot / Lowe's)** — only as last resort. Grass is usually fine; coneflower selection is hit-or-miss and often mislabeled; geraniums often unavailable.

## Pre-trip checklist

Before you leave for the nursery:

- [ ] Confirmed Karl Foerster availability by phone (it's standard but worth a call mid-season)
- [ ] Brought a tape measure (some nurseries sell #1 vs. #2 pots, sizes vary)
- [ ] Brought this list printed
- [ ] Truck or SUV with cargo space for 47 pots + 15+ bags of mulch/compost (or arranged delivery)
- [ ] Decided whether to do delivery (often $50–80 flat fee, worth it if you're buying bulk mulch and compost)

## After planting — first week purchases

Things you may not need until after install:
